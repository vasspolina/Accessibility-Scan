Position Poster

Type a chess game and some text. Each character becomes a piece in the final position; the squares behind it show how often each square was occupied during the game. Two looks: Classic sets the letters on a tinted board; Cut sets heavy capitals over colour bands and slices them, after the Museum für Gestaltung’s exhibition posters.

Game

Default: Topalov–Kasparov, Linares 2005, Kasparov’s last game. Paste any game; the position after the last move is used.

How the layout is built

The game is replayed move by move. The pieces left on the board after the last move give the title its squares: characters fill them in reading order, a8 to h1. Each square is scored by how many of the game’s positions it was occupied in, from the start position to the end.

Classic tints each square by that score and sets the info items on the same squares of a smaller board at the bottom. The destination of the last move is set in the accent colour.

Cut bleeds the board to the edges and lays three colour bands over it. The title is set in capitals, one per piece square; the more often the square was occupied, the more slices the letter is cut into and the further they slide, with a sliver of the opposite colour showing. Squares the game passed through but left empty carry a fragment of a title letter at twice the size, or a disc, half-disc or quarter cut. The info items are two small blocks, top left and right. The last move’s square is filled with the accent colour.
