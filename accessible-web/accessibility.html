Accessibility is a human right.No exceptions.

1/4 Touch

Life has moved online. We depend on the internet for work, connection, and daily necessities. It’s our entertainment, but it’s also essential. Everyone needs to be able to use it.

Remember: When you build a house, you don’t make the door optional. The same goes for digital access — everyone needs a way in.

Accessibility benefits everyone.

You’ve probably used accessible features without realizing it. Ever turn on captions while watching TV in a noisy room? That’s accessibility. Dictate a text message while driving? Also accessibility. Use voice commands when your hands are full? You get the idea.

These features weren’t designed exclusively for people with disabilities — they were designed for situations we all face. Curb cuts help wheelchair users, but they also help parents with strollers, travelers with luggage, and delivery workers with hand trucks.

Good accessible design doesn’t just help some users — it creates a better experience for everyone.

The legal landscape

As of June 28, 2025, accessibility isn’t optional anymore — it’s the law across Europe. Germany has the BFSG (Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz, the Accessibility Strengthening Act), the EU has the European Accessibility Act, and the UK enforces accessibility through the Equality Act 2010. All of these require websites to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA standards.

What happens if you don’t comply? Fines can reach up to €100,000 in Germany, and over €1,000,000 in some EU countries.

Plus, consumers and advocacy groups can request inspections and take legal action if accessibility problems aren’t fixed.

Who needs accessibility?

Here’s the thing: accessibility isn’t just for people with permanent disabilities. At some point, we all need these features.

Motor limitations
Broken arms, carrying groceries, holding a baby
Visual limitations
Forgot your glasses, cracked phone screen, sun glare on your laptop
Auditory limitations
Loud café, ear infection, dead headphone battery
Cognitive limitations
Tired after a long day, stressed and distracted, trying to multitask

Sound familiar? That’s because accessible design helps all of us.

What makes a website accessible?

Accessible websites follow four core principles from the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG):

Perceivable
Everyone can access your information, even if they can’t use all their senses
Operable
All your website’s functions work for everyone, no exceptions
Understandable
Your content is clear and your navigation makes sense
Robust
Your site works on any device with any assistive technology, today, tomorrow, and for the foreseeable future

Does this apply to my business?

Short answer: Probably yes.

If you operate in the EU and your website does any of the following, you need to comply:

  • Sells products or services online
  • Allows users to book appointments or reservations
  • Provides banking, transport, or media services
  • Was updated or published after June 28, 2025

This includes e-commerce sites, booking platforms, SaaS companies, restaurants with online ordering, and more.

The only exemption: Companies with fewer than 10 employees and annual revenue under €2 million.

But even small businesses aren’t always exempt. If you sell products like smartphones, tablets, or e-readers, you must comply regardless of company size.
