Copied here because a doc that governs every string in every locale should
be readable beside the strings, not only by whoever has the Drive link.

It governs ALL copy in ALL locales. Where it and a locale brief disagree
about typography, the locale brief wins for that locale (German takes a
spaced en dash where this doc takes an em dash) — everything else holds.

**Not the same as `docs/VOICE.md`.** That one governs findings text, which
is deliberately flatter: short title, precise, jargon-free, no metaphor,
no wit. Restraint is the voice inside a report. This doc is the site and
product voice.

## Tone essence

A design studio that speaks human. Fluent in code, culture, and common
sense.

Clear, informed, quietly witty — a studio that takes accessibility
seriously but not solemnly. Precision (design systems and compliance)
balanced with warmth (people). Confident without posturing, educational
without preaching.

> If we sound like a person, it's the person in your design meeting who
> knows the law, fixes your layout, and still makes you laugh.

## Voice pillars

**1. Intelligent simplicity.** Write like we design: clean, intentional,
stripped of noise. Short, declarative, slightly rhythmic. Jargon gets
translated, not repeated.
> "Accessibility isn't a feature. It's the foundation."

Avoid: verbosity, marketing fluff, filler transitions.

**2. Human precision.** Technical, never robotic. Complex things should
sound like a designer walking you through them — precise, with empathy.
> "AI can spot missing alt text, but it can't tell 'K-pop band posing with
> fans' from 'group of people.'"

Avoid: bureaucratic tone, passive voice, empty authority.

**3. Understated humor.** Dry, human wit. Metaphor the way designers use
contrast — to make the point sharper, not louder.
> "Retrofitting an old site is like trying to make a flip phone run
> Instagram."

Avoid: sarcasm, quirkiness, overextended jokes.

**4. Moral clarity.** Accessibility as shared responsibility, not a favour.
Assume empathy and intelligence in the reader.
> "Accessibility isn't about compliance — it's about equality."

Avoid: moralizing, pity, fear-based urgency.

## Calibration

| Dimension | Lean | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Informal | toward formal | Professional but conversational. A design expert explaining clearly, not a government PDF. Plain language, no slang. |
| Practical ↔ Poetic | toward poetic | Concrete, with metaphor where it sharpens meaning. |
| Serious ↔ Playful | toward serious | Seriousness from conviction, not tone. A light wink, never at the expense of clarity. |
| Technical ↔ Human | centre-human | WCAG translated into everyday language. |
| Directive ↔ Reflective | centre-reflective | Guidance over command. Why before what. |
| Corporate ↔ Cultural | centre-cultural | Design culture, not corporate marketing. |
| Advocacy ↔ Education | centre-education | Inform, don't lecture. Design literacy, not moral superiority. |

## By channel

**Website** — like architecture: structured, clear, elegant. Even,
confident rhythm. Metaphors serve design thinking (door, foundation,
house). Authority implied through clarity, not volume.

**Newsletter** — educational, warm, lightly humorous. Explain, don't
advertise. Metaphor anchors abstract concepts.

**Social** — minimal, declarative, visual-first. Each post a distilled
poster or aphorism. Humour sly and intelligent, closer to editorial irony
than meme.

## Stylistic rules

- Sentence case for all headings, captions and UI text
- Sentences under 20 words, ideally rhythmic
- Paragraphs of at most three sentences
- Lists to simplify technical information
- Punctuation for rhythm and clarity, not drama
- No exclamation points (ever)
- Em dashes may replace parentheses — never overused
- Lists clean and parallel, no full stops at the end of items
- Dates in European format (28 June 2025)
- First person plural, used naturally and sparingly — collective, not
  corporate

## Two notes from applying it

- The doc's own website example measures a door at 5'10". Imperial units
  must not survive into a non-English locale; the German brief says so
  outright. Convert, don't carry (1,78 m).
- "No overpromise" is not in the stylistic list but runs through the
  pillars, and both locale briefs make it explicit: a site can be
  compliant; no site is fully accessible.
